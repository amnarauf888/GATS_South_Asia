# GATS South Asia: Anti-Tobacco Media Exposure and Cessation

Pooled cross-country analysis of the Global Adult Tobacco Survey (GATS), examining whether exposure to anti-tobacco media and health warning labels is associated with quit attempts and quit intentions among smokers and smokeless tobacco (SLT) users across South Asia.

## Background

Tobacco use is among the leading causes of preventable death and disability globally, and South Asia has ranked first in the world for oral cavity cancer burden since 1990, with an age-standardized DALY rate that grew 13% to reach 197 per 100,000 in 2023. Smokeless tobacco (SLT) and smoking together account for a substantial share of this burden. Anti-tobacco media and health warning labels represent a key FCTC-mandated intervention for driving cessation, yet quit rates remain low, and evidence on their effectiveness across both smokers and SLT users in South Asia remains limited.

## Data

GATS data were pooled across four South Asian countries, harmonizing tobacco user classification (smoker only, SLT only, dual user), exposure construction, and covariate categories identically across countries.

|-------------|-------------|--------------------|
| Pakistan    | 2014        | 1,555              |
| India       | 2016–2017   | 21,855             |
| Bangladesh  | 2017        | 5,128              |
| Sri Lanka   | 2019        | 1,157              |

## Methods

Survey-weighted logistic regression estimated associations between anti-smoking and anti-SLT media exposure and two outcomes — quit attempts and quit intentions — adjusting for age, gender, education, residence, employment, dual-use status, and (in pooled models) country.

## Results

In the pooled analysis, anti-smoking media exposure was associated with higher odds of both quit attempts (aOR = 1.56, 95% CI: 1.27–1.91) and quit intentions (aOR = 1.28, 95% CI: 1.02–1.61) among smokers. Anti-SLT exposure was associated with higher odds of quit attempts (aOR = 1.49, 95% CI: 1.29–1.72) but not quit intentions (aOR = 1.11, 95% CI: 0.94–1.32) among SLT users.

Country-stratified models showed significant quit-attempt associations in Pakistan (smoking aOR = 2.07; SLT aOR = 2.70) and India (smoking aOR = 1.49; SLT aOR = 1.45), but not in Bangladesh or Sri Lanka. India was the only country showing a significant quit-intention association for either product, specifically for smoking (aOR = 1.35, 95% CI: 1.03–1.77).

## Conclusions

Anti-tobacco media exposure is consistently associated with quit attempts across South Asia. In India, this association also extends to quit intention for smoking — an exception to the attempt-only pattern seen in Pakistan, Bangladesh, and Sri Lanka.

Because exposure was measured over the past 30 days and quit attempts over the past 12 months, this study cannot establish temporal precedence or causal direction; reverse causality remains a plausible alternative explanation for the quit attempt-only pattern. Quit intention does not carry this same problem, since it reflects a person's current state rather than something that may have happened months earlier. India's exception may instead reflect its more sustained and wide-reaching anti-tobacco media environment relative to the other three countries.
